The portraits in **40 anys de blues a Barcelona** were made in the studio, with an analogue camera, black-and-white film and hand processing. Each musician stood in front of the lens for one day, in a portrait session agreed without haste, and what has survived is the negative: not one photograph in this archive has been through digital retouching.

## What large format photography is

Large format photography uses negatives far bigger than those of medium or small format: 4×5, 5×7 or 8×10 inches, that is, between 100 and 500 times the area of a 35 mm frame. The bigger the negative, the more information it carries: more detail in the midtones, more shades of grey and a texture unlike anything a digital sensor produces.

In exchange, large format is slow. The camera sits on a tripod, the viewfinder is covered with a dark cloth, each sheet is loaded by hand and the photographer has to settle the exposure before releasing the shutter. That slowness is precisely what matters in a portrait: it forces you to look at the musician, to talk to them and to wait for the gesture.

## Medium format and large format in the archive

The project combines two formats. Most of the portraits were made in **6×6 medium format** with a Hasselblad 500 CM, a 1957 camera that gives a six-by-six centimetre square and forces you to compose inside a closed frame. It is the most agile format for a studio portrait session and the one that gives the archive its visual unity.

A smaller group of portraits was made in **8×10 large format** with a Horseman, using the largest negative that can be handled comfortably inside a studio. Those are the slowest sessions in the whole project and the ones that allow the portrait to be enlarged to wall size without losing anything.

## The process: film, development and printing

Every session used fine-grain black-and-white film —Rollei RPX 100, Ilford HP5 and Foma 100 and 400— developed by hand with Rodinal, working up the density each film needs for a portrait. The result is a raw negative, with visible grain and contained highlights, the opposite of retouching: nothing is hidden here.

Chemical photography is therefore a way of working and also a way of looking. The blues was born as a poor music, made with simple instruments and whatever was at hand; the second-hand camera, the out-of-date film and the home darkroom are the same gesture applied to the image.

## The sessions and the people in them

The 148 profiles in the archive carry the date of the portrait session, made in the Barcelona studio between 2020 and 2026. Each profile holds the portrait, the biography written by [Manuel López Poy](/en/autors/manuel-lopez-poy/) and the bands the musician has played with.
