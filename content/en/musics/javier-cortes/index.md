His contact with the blues came by intellectual means during his adolescent days: “It happened on reading a feature about the influence of the devil on twentieth-century music. Among bands with lots of hair, satanic imagery, studs and leather, I found the photo of a gentleman in an elegant suit and hat, smiling at the camera with a guitar. It was [Robert Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Johnson_(musician)) and his story fascinated and unsettled me in equal measure”. He embraced that music with the same enthusiasm with which he had embraced that of the [Ramones](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramones) or the [Clash](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clash). In 2013, three years after settling in Barcelona, he began to take to the stage professionally, letting himself be caught by roots music: “As the years have gone by, I've realised that all the music I like or that moves me pivots around the blues. I suppose I never deliberately decided to become a musician in the genre, it just happened naturally”. For this forceful double bassist with a [rockabilly](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly) soul, the blues has given him a sense of belonging to a musical community, and it seduces him for its universality, its complex simplicity, its social history and its cathartic power: “The blues can make you cry, laugh or raise your body temperature”.
