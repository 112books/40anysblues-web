He began playing the harmonica at the age of twelve, while blues and jazz records played at home, among them one by [Muddy Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muddy_Waters) that particularly caught his ear. In the mid-nineties he began playing with groups of friends in any venue that would have them. Starting out from [Chicago blues](https://ca.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_blues), he let himself be seduced by the rawness and visceral quality of the [Chess Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Records) musicians, eventually reaching far broader registers: “From the most primitive blues, drawn to a blend of folk and country that I found rhythmically more complex, to the more modern blues of [Johnny Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Winter) or [Stevie Ray Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Ray_Vaughan), who had a more rocking vibe that seduced me for its force, its sound.” Having become a virtuoso of the chromatic harmonica, at the end of the first decade of the twenty-first century he began a new phase as a double bassist that has made him an indispensable member of numerous bands. An artist of jovial character and an intelligent sense of humour, he is grateful to his profession as a musician for a different relationship with life: “A lot of laughter with my colleagues, meeting all kinds of people, many hours in the car, many... ha, ha. And perhaps what I am most grateful for is having been able to be in different villages, cities and countries that I certainly would not have been able to visit with another kind of job.”
