She arrived in Barcelona at the age of twenty-two with the firm determination to become a blues harmonica player, something she achieved through dedication, hard work and talent, becoming the only female exponent of the instrument in this country, as well as a member of the only band made up exclusively of women, [Índigo Blues](/en/bands/%C3%ADndigo-blues/). To put it graphically, in very few years she went from being part of the regular audience at the [Honky Tonk](/en/sales/honky-tonk-blues-bar/) to shining from its stage. Her relationship with the blues was a resounding love at first sight that turned this music into the soundtrack of her life. She began performing professionally in 2014, when she met the guitarist [Johnny Big Stone](/en/musics/johnny-big-stone/), with whom she created Sweet Marta & The Blues Workers (who later became [Blues Shakers](/en/bands/sweet-marta--the-blues-shakers/)) and recorded her first album. A virtuoso of the harmonica, with a voice laden with energy and sensitivity at the same time, she has become a regular figure at Spanish festivals and is beginning to tread the European stages with confidence, with a repertoire that combines, with total assurance, the most primitive rural blues with the electrified blues of Chicago or rhythm&blues.
