Radio was the window through which the blues slipped into her life when she was little, and she guessed that what Los Deltonos played — “the only rhythm&blues that came on the radio”, in her own words — was the rhythm she liked best. She began singing among friends, who encouraged her to polish her voice and steered her towards the [Taller de Músics](/en/sales/taller-de-musics/), where she fell for ever, utterly won over, to the devil’s music. “The die was already cast.” She began going up on stage in 1991, and after a promising start and a six-year break she returned to singing blues and jazz, but this time with a new weapon added to her magnificent voice: the electric bass, which allowed her to venture into more rock-oriented musical territory, such as that of the band Cassay, an all-female rock outfit. An artist of character and determination, she decided to put together a blues band with rocky touches and confessed that she was caught by a genre that allowed her to express all the music she carried inside, even though at times everything seemed to be against her: “My whole childhood and youth were surrounded by circumstances that insisted on drawing me away from music; the blues bored through the asphalt that imprisoned me and made my true self blossom.”
